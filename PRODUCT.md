# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Pastors, church staff, informed volunteers, website professionals, and project facilitators using an AI agent to create or improve a church website. They may begin with no preparation and need to know what to do now, what information matters, what can remain unknown, and what decision or approval comes next.

## Product Purpose

Church Website Blueprint gives people and their chosen AI agent a dependable process for turning current church knowledge, interviews, and existing materials into source-faithful website strategy, content, design, implementation, and launch decisions. Success means a first-time facilitator can move through the process without reading repository documentation, guessing the next step, or accidentally inventing church information.

## Brand Personality

Clear, capable, calm. The interface should feel practical and trustworthy, use plain English, and make a complex process feel finite and manageable.

## Anti-references

- A documentation portal that exposes the repository structure instead of guiding the current task.
- A large questionnaire or content library that asks people to decide where to begin.
- Generic church-marketing language, persuasive promises, or implied ecclesial preferences.
- Decorative cards, repeated labels, and visual flourishes that compete with the next action.
- Workflows that assume preparation, hide completion criteria, or leave the facilitator unsure what happens next.

## Design Principles

1. Show the next required action before optional depth.
2. Make every sequence, branch, stopping point, and handoff explicit.
3. Use progressive disclosure so the full framework is available without appearing all at once.
4. Treat unknown information as trackable project state, not user failure.
5. Keep people in the human-facing guide; repository files support the agent behind the scenes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA as the minimum. Maintain strong contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic headings and controls, large tap targets, reduced-motion support, readable line lengths, and instructions that never depend on color alone. Assume mixed technical confidence, church roles, ages, abilities, and recording environments.
