# Run it with people

<p class="page-lead">The process is designed to begin with a conversation, not a homework packet. One person can facilitate it for a church, or two people at the same church can work through it together.</p>

## 1. Start the project

Complete the Project Launch Sheet live. Determine whether the church has no site, an existing site, or a bounded improvement need. Identify who knows the church and who can approve facts, theology, design, and launch.

## 2. Use what already exists

If a website exists, assess it before the main discovery interview. Capture current pages, claims, assets, systems, contradictions, and missing visitor information. The goal is not to judge the church's preferences. The goal is to see whether different visitors can understand the church and determine fit.

## 3. Have the next useful conversation

Use a short Conversation Brief generated from what is still unknown. Ask broad opening questions, listen carefully, and use probes only where they uncover a consequential gap.

## 4. Reflect back what you heard

After the transcript is processed, send a What We Heard summary. Participants can correct a claim, confirm it, assign it to someone else, keep it private, or identify a decision that still needs to be made.

## 5. Repeat only when needed

The next step may be another interview, but it may also be a document request, a specialist answer, a church decision, or approval. The process adapts rather than forcing everyone through every possible question.

## 6. Approve one milestone at a time

- Foundation/DNA
- Visitor strategy and sitemap
- Page copy
- Visual direction
- Launch readiness

Each packet asks one clear approval question. Nobody should be asked to “review the whole project” at once.

## 7. Hand off ownership

Before launch, name primary and backup administrators, content owners, form recipients, review cadence, and escalation paths.

<div class="callout"><strong>The human experience is a guided series of conversations and decisions.</strong> The ledger, source maps, and project state run underneath it.</div>
