<section class="section purpose-section">
  <div class="section-split">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow section-eyebrow">What this tool does</p>
      <h2>It turns what your church knows into a website people can actually use.</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="plain-summary">
      <p>Instead of starting with a theme or a blank page, you start with evidence: interviews, your current website, ministry information, photographs, documents, and the people who know how the church really works.</p>
      <p>The framework tells an AI agent what information to collect, how to structure it, what it may and may not infer, and which artifact should come next. It can work with Codex, Claude Code, OpenCode, OpenClaw, Hermes, or another agentic system that can read files and follow project instructions.</p>
      <p><strong>The agent runs the framework. People remain the source of truth and approve the work.</strong></p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="outcome-grid" aria-label="What the process is designed to improve">
    <div><strong>Visitor clarity</strong><span>People can quickly understand the church, decide whether it may fit, and know what to do next.</span></div>
    <div><strong>Search readiness</strong><span>Pages have clear jobs, useful information, sensible structure, and content that answers real questions.</span></div>
    <div><strong>Truthful content</strong><span>Copy comes from approved interviews and source material instead of generic church language.</span></div>
    <div><strong>Long-term ownership</strong><span>The process names who maintains service details, ministries, forms, events, and other changing information.</span></div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section prep-section">
  <div class="section-heading-row">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow section-eyebrow">Before you begin</p>
      <h2>You only need five things to start.</h2>
    </div>
    <p class="section-intro">Bring more if you have it. Do not delay the first conversation because your files are incomplete.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="needs-grid">
    <article class="need-item need-item-agent"><span>01</span><div><h3>An AI agent that can work with files</h3><p>Use the agentic system you prefer. It needs to read the toolkit, create and update project documents, and follow instructions across multiple stages.</p></div></article>
    <article class="need-item"><span>02</span><div><h3>Someone who knows the church</h3><p>A pastor, staff member, leader, or informed volunteer who can describe the church and point to the right approvers.</p></div></article>
    <article class="need-item"><span>03</span><div><h3>A way to record and transcribe</h3><p>Record interviews with permission. Use any reliable meeting, recording, or transcription tool that identifies speakers and preserves the full conversation.</p></div></article>
    <article class="need-item"><span>04</span><div><h3>A private place for project files</h3><p>Use a private shared folder or private repository for transcripts, internal decisions, contact information, analytics, and unpublished content.</p></div></article>
    <article class="need-item"><span>05</span><div><h3>Someone who can approve the work</h3><p>Know who can confirm facts, theology, visual direction, technical choices, and the final launch—even if those are different people.</p></div></article>
  </div>
  <details class="bring-list">
    <summary>Helpful things to bring if they already exist</summary>
    <div>
      <ul>
        <li>Current website URL and any known problem pages</li>
        <li>Analytics or search performance reports</li>
        <li>Mission, beliefs, ministry, newcomer, and membership materials</li>
        <li>Service times, locations, staff information, forms, and system links</li>
      </ul>
      <ul>
        <li>Logos, colors, fonts, brand guidelines, and print pieces</li>
        <li>Original photography or video with permission to publish</li>
        <li>Domain, hosting, website platform, and integration information</li>
        <li>Examples of websites the church likes or dislikes</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </details>
</section>

<section class="section partnership-section">
  <div class="section-heading-row">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow section-eyebrow">One process, two responsibilities</p>
      <h2>The human and the agent work through the same project together.</h2>
    </div>
    <p class="section-intro">The website is written for the people directing the project. The toolkit files give the AI agent the detailed operating instructions underneath that experience.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="responsibility-grid">
    <article>
      <span>People</span>
      <h3>Know, decide, and approve</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Explain how the church actually works</li>
        <li>Supply documents, links, systems, and media</li>
        <li>Identify uncertainty and private information</li>
        <li>Make strategic and operational decisions</li>
        <li>Approve facts, theology, copy, design, and launch</li>
      </ul>
    </article>
    <article>
      <span>AI agent</span>
      <h3>Organize, question, and create</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Read the framework and persistent project state</li>
        <li>Assess existing evidence and prepare interviews</li>
        <li>Structure transcripts without inventing facts</li>
        <li>Track gaps, contradictions, decisions, and approvals</li>
        <li>Produce DNA, strategy, copy, design, and QA artifacts</li>
      </ul>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-dark process-guide">
  <div class="section-inner">
    <p class="eyebrow section-eyebrow">The simple flow</p>
    <h2>Go from “where do we start?” to a website you can launch.</h2>
    <p class="section-intro">The process adapts to a new site, redesign, or focused improvement. You do not have to complete every possible question before useful work begins.</p>
    <ol class="guide-steps">
      <li><span>01</span><div><h3>Point your agent to the toolkit</h3><p>Give it the repository and setup prompt. It reads the skill, creates a private workspace, and asks only what it needs to begin.</p></div></li>
      <li><span>02</span><div><h3>Choose your starting point</h3><p>Decide whether you are creating a new site, redesigning an existing one, or improving one defined area.</p></div></li>
      <li><span>03</span><div><h3>Gather what already exists</h3><p>Collect useful pages, documents, photographs, brand files, systems, and search or analytics evidence. If little exists, move on.</p></div></li>
      <li><span>04</span><div><h3>Have the first recorded conversation</h3><p>Use the agent-prepared brief to clarify the church, its visitors, the Sunday experience, beliefs, ministries, next steps, and constraints.</p></div></li>
      <li><span>05</span><div><h3>Let the agent organize—not invent</h3><p>The agent turns the transcript into confirmed facts, open questions, decisions, and focused DNA documents. Missing information becomes a follow-up question.</p></div></li>
      <li><span>06</span><div><h3>Plan, write, and design</h3><p>Use approved information to create the visitor strategy, sitemap, page briefs, public copy, visual system, and layouts.</p></div></li>
      <li><span>07</span><div><h3>Build, test, launch, and assign owners</h3><p>Check visitor journeys, forms, accessibility, search fundamentals, factual accuracy, privacy, redirects, and ongoing maintenance.</p></div></li>
    </ol>
