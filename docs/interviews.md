# Ask the questions that unlock the next useful step

<p class="page-lead">The interview library covers the whole church website project, but it is not a questionnaire to finish in one sitting. Use the project’s biggest unanswered questions to prepare a short, natural conversation.</p>

<div class="quick-start">
  <strong>Do not ask everything at once</strong>
  <p>For each interview, choose about five to eight high-value questions. Begin broadly, listen carefully, and use optional probes only where an answer needs more clarity.</p>

</div>

## Before you record

<div class="check-grid">
  <div><span>✓</span><p>Get permission to record and explain how the transcript will be used.</p></div>
  <div><span>✓</span><p>Identify each speaker, their role, and what they are authorized to approve.</p></div>
  <div><span>✓</span><p>Keep the recording and transcript in the private church workspace.</p></div>
  <div><span>✓</span><p>Know the purpose of this conversation and the decision or document it should unlock.</p></div>
  <div><span>✓</span><p>Bring forward what is already known so people do not have to repeat existing material.</p></div>
  <div><span>✓</span><p>Mark opinions, aspirations, uncertainty, sensitive information, and approval needs as they appear.</p></div>
</div>

## Begin with the launch conversation

If the project is new or still unclear, these questions are enough to begin:

<ol class="question-list">
  <li><span>01</span><p>Are we creating a new website, redesigning an existing one, or improving one defined part?</p></li>
  <li><span>02</span><p>Why is this project happening now?</p></li>
  <li><span>03</span><p>What already exists: a website, documents, brand materials, photography, analytics, or systems?</p></li>
  <li><span>04</span><p>Who knows the church well enough to participate?</p></li>
  <li><span>05</span><p>Who can approve facts, theology, visual direction, technology, and launch?</p></li>
  <li><span>06</span><p>Which visitors or church needs matter most for this project?</p></li>
  <li><span>07</span><p>What constraints are already known: timing, budget, platform, staffing, privacy, or access?</p></li>
  <li><span>08</span><p>What is the next concrete action, who owns it, and what should it produce?</p></li>
</ol>

## Use three conversations as a starting pattern

<div class="session-grid">
  <article><span>Session A</span><h3>Identity and visitor promise</h3><p>Audience, desired outcomes, church identity, story, mission, values, beliefs, culture, and voice.</p></article>
  <article><span>Session B</span><h3>Experience and operations</h3><p>The Sunday journey, services, ministries, next steps, leaders, media, events, giving, care, systems, and maintenance.</p></article>
  <article><span>Session C</span><h3>Verification and decisions</h3><p>Correct names and facts, resolve contradictions, separate present truth from aspiration, approve sensitive wording, and assign owners.</p></article>
</div>

<p>Two conversations of about 90 minutes plus a shorter verification call is a useful default—not a rule. A focused improvement may need one short conversation. A complex church may need several people or specialist follow-ups.</p>

## Ask natural follow-up questions

These simple probes are useful throughout the conversation:

- “Can you give me a real example?”
- “Is that true now, or is that where you want to go?”
- “Who can confirm that?”
- “May that be published?”
- “Would every leader describe it the same way?”
- “What would a first-time visitor need explained?”
- “What happens immediately before and after that?”
- “Who owns this when it changes?”

## After every interview

1. Save the recording and transcript in the private source folder.
2. Extract individual claims without adding facts that were not stated.
3. Mark uncertainty, contradictions, decisions, aspirations, and private material.
4. Update the topic coverage and project state.
5. Send a short **What We Heard + Open Questions** summary for correction.
6. Decide the best next action: another interview, a document request, a specialist answer, a church decision, or approval.
