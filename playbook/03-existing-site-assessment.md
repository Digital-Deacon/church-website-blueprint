# Existing-Site Visitor-Fit Assessment

## Purpose

Assess what an existing church website communicates before asking people to recreate that information in interviews. The assessment answers:

> Can different visitors quickly understand this church, decide whether it may be a good fit, and know what to do next?

This is not a ranking of churches, theology, worship preferences, or ministry models. A church can make choices that are not right for every visitor and still communicate them clearly and honestly.

## Assessment principles

1. **Evidence before judgment.** Every finding names the page, element, or missing path that produced it.
2. **Clarity before preference.** The assessment asks whether a visitor can understand the church's choice, not whether the reviewer prefers it.
3. **Journey before page polish.** A beautiful page can still fail if someone cannot answer a practical question or find the next step.
4. **Multiple lenses.** Different visitors need different information, but the site should not become a maze of persona-specific pages.
5. **Current truth.** Conflicting or apparently stale information lowers confidence even when one version is correct.
6. **Research and church facts remain separate.** General visitor research may inform the rubric; it never supplies facts about this church.

## Inputs

Use whatever is available:

- public website and all discoverable pages;
- navigation, footer, search, forms, downloads, and embedded systems;
- mobile and desktop renderings;
- metadata, structured data, performance, and accessibility results;
- public sermon, event, giving, social, map, and business-profile links;
- analytics or Search Console data when authorized;
- known project scope and constraints.

## Step 1 — Inventory the current site

Capture:

- URL and page title;
- navigation location;
- apparent page purpose;
- primary audience and CTA;
- major claims and operational facts;
- content owner/source if discoverable;
- last-updated signal;
- forms, downloads, embeds, and external destinations;
- reusable text and media assets;
- status: `KEEP`, `IMPROVE`, `MERGE`, `REMOVE`, or `DECIDE`.

Do not make final keep/remove decisions during automated inventory. Record a preliminary recommendation and the evidence behind it.

## Step 2 — Test the universal visitor questions

The site should let a visitor answer these without extensive research:

1. Who is this church?
2. Where is it?
3. When does it meet?
4. What should I expect at a service?
5. What is available for my children or teenagers?
6. What does the church believe and, when relevant, what tradition or affiliation does it belong to?
7. What is the worship/service environment like?
8. What accessibility, language, or special-arrival information matters?
9. How can I watch or listen before attending, if available?
10. What should I do next if I want to visit, ask a question, or become involved?

Not every answer belongs on the homepage. The test is whether the path is obvious and the answer is proportionate to its importance.

## Step 3 — Score each visitor task

Use a 0–4 scale for each dimension. Scores create comparability across iterations; evidence and consequences matter more than the total.

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Absent or impossible to complete |
| 1 | Present only through guesswork, external searching, or conflicting information |
| 2 | Findable, but incomplete, unclear, poorly placed, or difficult on common devices |
| 3 | Clear and complete with minor friction |
| 4 | Immediate, specific, confidence-building, and connected to the next step |

### Dimensions

| Dimension | Test |
|---|---|
| Findability | Can the visitor locate the answer from a plausible entry point? |
| Clarity | Can an outsider understand the language and meaning? |
| Completeness | Is there enough information to make the relevant decision? |
| Confidence | Does the information appear specific, current, consistent, and trustworthy? |
| Fit transparency | Can the visitor understand the church's actual identity, practices, and offerings without promotional fog? |
| Hierarchy | Is the most important information presented before secondary detail? |
| Next-step continuity | After learning the answer, is the appropriate action obvious and functional? |

## Step 4 — Run the visitor lenses

Personas are lenses, not fictional market segments to stereotype. Each lens asks a bounded set of questions and reports what the actual site reveals.

### Lens A — Exploring church or Christianity

Primary questions:

- Can I understand the church without knowing insider vocabulary?
- Will I know what will happen and whether I will be singled out?
- Is there a low-pressure way to ask a question, watch, or visit?
- Does the site explain rather than assume beliefs and practices?

### Lens B — Christian new to the area

Primary questions:

- Can I quickly understand mission, beliefs, worship, leadership, and affiliation?
- Can I hear teaching before visiting?
- Can I see credible paths toward community, discipleship, and service?

### Lens C — Parent or guardian of infants and young children

Primary questions:

- What ages are served during each service?
- How do check-in, safety, pickup, allergies, and first-time arrival work?
- Can I determine whether children stay in the service or attend separate programming?
- Is there an obvious contact for a specific concern?

### Lens D — Household with school-age children and teenagers

Primary questions:

- Which programs exist for each age or grade?
- When and where do they meet?
- What do they actually do, and who leads them?
- Are the details current enough to plan around?

### Lens E — Theology- or denomination-conscious visitor

Primary questions:

- Is there an approved statement of faith or meaningful summary?
- Is denominational/network affiliation clear?
- Can I understand relevant practices such as baptism, communion, governance, leadership, or membership?
- Does the site use precise language rather than generic claims?

### Lens F — Visitor with accessibility, language, or care needs

Primary questions:

- Can I determine whether the building and service are accessible to me?
- Are interpretation, hearing, sensory, mobility, or other supports described when available?
- Can I find a safe, appropriate contact route without disclosing sensitive information publicly?

Optional lenses may cover online-only participants, college students, older adults, military families, community-service seekers, or other groups supported by project evidence.

## Step 5 — Assess information architecture and hierarchy

Review:

- whether navigation labels make sense outside the church;
- whether important paths are visible without scanning every menu item;
- whether duplicated pages disagree;
- whether pages have one clear job;
- whether headings expose the structure of long pages;
- whether calls to action match the page's job;
- whether current members' utility needs obscure the first-visit journey;
- whether mobile navigation preserves the same clarity;
- whether users must remember facts from one page to act on another.

Flag decision points with more than four comparable choices and content areas that lack progressive disclosure.

## Step 6 — Assess credibility and freshness

Look for:

- conflicting service times, addresses, staff names, or contact information;
- expired events or seasonal messages;
- ministries presented as active without current evidence;
- broken forms, downloads, embeds, maps, or social links;
- missing dates where recency matters;
- generic claims unsupported by concrete examples;
- unowned pages that appear abandoned;
- privacy or photo-use concerns;
- theology summaries that differ from linked authoritative statements.

Treat each observation as a verification question until the church confirms it.

## Step 7 — Extract reusable evidence

The assessment should not only criticize. It should prepopulate discovery with:

- candidate facts for the fact ledger;
- existing mission, belief, story, ministry, service, and contact language;
- useful photographs, logos, video, sermons, and downloads;
- current navigation and URLs;
- integration destinations and form recipients when visible;
- language that appears distinctive to the church;
- content that appears approved or canonical;
- contradictions and age-sensitive facts requiring verification.

Publicly visible information is still `NEEDS_CONFIRMATION` until the church verifies that it is current and approved.

## Priority levels

- `P0` — dangerous, materially misleading, privacy/security issue, or blocks essential action.
- `P1` — prevents a core visitor from determining fit or completing the primary next step.
- `P2` — creates meaningful confusion, distrust, or avoidable effort.
- `P3` — polish, efficiency, or future improvement.

## Reassessment

Persist each assessment. After redesign, run the same tasks and lenses again. Compare dimension scores, but also report whether previous journey-breaking issues have actually disappeared. A higher visual-quality score does not compensate for missing service, belief, children, or next-step information.
