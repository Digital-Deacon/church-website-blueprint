# Existing-Site Assessment

## Purpose

Determine what the current site already communicates and where visitors cannot confidently understand the church, determine fit, or act.

Do not score whether the church matches a reviewer's preferences. Score clarity and journey performance.

## Pass 1: capture evidence

Inventory:

- URLs, titles, navigation, and apparent page jobs;
- claims, service facts, beliefs, ministries, contacts, and calls to action;
- forms, downloads, embeds, maps, giving, events, sermons, and external systems;
- reusable copy and media;
- stale, conflicting, missing, or unowned content;
- likely redirects and preserved URLs.

Treat public claims as `NEEDS_CONFIRMATION` until verified.

## Pass 2: test visitor tasks

Test whether a visitor can find and understand:

- church identity;
- location and service times;
- service expectations and worship environment;
- children and youth information;
- beliefs and affiliation;
- accessibility or language information;
- sermons or online participation when offered;
- contact and next steps.

Assess findability, clarity, completeness, confidence, fit transparency, hierarchy, and next-step continuity on a 0–4 scale. Preserve evidence for every finding.

Use relevant lenses: exploring Christianity, Christian new to the area, young-child household, school-age/youth household, theology-conscious visitor, and accessibility/care needs.

## Keep evidence passes distinct

Separate visitor/content assessment from objective technical and accessibility checks. Synthesize them afterward; do not let a technical score replace journey judgment.

## Output

Create an Existing-Site Findings Brief containing:

- scope and limitations;
- what is already clear;
- highest-impact gaps;
- visitor-lens results;
- task scorecard with evidence;
- reusable assets and candidate facts;
- conflicts and stale signals;
- recommended interview questions.

Update the coverage matrix and project state.
